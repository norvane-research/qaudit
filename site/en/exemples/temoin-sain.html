In plain words: what this report says

Green light. The results provided passed all five statistical tests and no serious flaw was found in the way the trading is done. This means the observed performance is neither explained by chance, nor carried by a few lucky trades, nor manufactured by questionable data — over the period and data provided.

The verdict covers only what was given to the auditor: the closed trades in the file, over the period covered. It rests on no human judgement and no overall score: every test has a threshold fixed in advance by the method, and the verdict follows mechanically from those thresholds. The same file, audited again tomorrow with the same version, yields exactly the same report.

The five questions put to the results, and the numeric answer for this file:

passed
Is chance ruled out?

When several versions of a strategy are tried before one is kept, the best one always looks good, even if none has an edge. This test computes the probability that the performance is real once that effect is corrected, for 10 declared variant(s): 97.6 %. Pass threshold: 50.0 %. Test passed.

passed
And with bad luck?

The profit factor is the total won divided by the total lost (1.00 = break-even). The same trades were replayed 10 000 times in a different order, in blocks, to see what the same strategy would yield with less luck: in the 5 % least favourable scenarios the profit factor falls to 1.187. Threshold: 1.00 — below it, the strategy loses money in those scenarios. Test passed.

passed
Do the losses come clustered?

The drawdown is the largest fall between a peak and the trough that follows it. The observed one (2 042.87) is larger than in 78.1 % of random reorderings of the same trades. A very low value (under 5 %) would signal a curve too smooth to be honest; a very high value means the losses came clustered, which is harder to live through than the final result suggests. Test passed.

passed
Would a system with no edge do as well?

10 000 systems with no edge at all, but winning and losing amounts of the same size as this one, were simulated; 0.0 % of them did as well or better. Threshold: 5.0 % — above it, this system cannot be told apart from a system with no edge. Test passed.

passed
Does it hold outside the periods it was tuned on?

A strategy tuned on one period tends to shine there and disappoint afterwards. This test compares its performance on periods not used for tuning: it keeps 65.4 % of it. Threshold: 40.0 %. Test passed.

No serious flaw was found in the way the trading is done: no martingale, no grid, no abnormal concentration of profit, no sign of tampered data.

What remains true despite the green light: a statistical test measures the past that was provided, not the future. The drawdown figures in the “Benchmarks” section show the size of temporary losses it is reasonable to expect from this system as it was observed.

This report states facts; it recommends no settings and predicts nothing.

Probabilistic and deflated Sharpe

How to read this sectionThe Sharpe ratio measures the steadiness of gains. The PSR is the probability that the true Sharpe is positive given the number of trades and the shape of the results. The DSR answers the harder question: does this performance exceed what chance would have given to the best of N attempts? If the requester tried more variants than declared, the true DSR is lower than the one shown.

PSR: probability that the true Sharpe is positive, corrected for skewness and fat tails. DSR: the same probability evaluated against the best Sharpe expected by chance after N trials.

Resampling

How to read this sectionThree simulations on the same trades, for three questions. Bootstrap: with less luck, what profit and what drawdown? (the p05 / p50 / p95 ranges bracket 90 % of scenarios). Permutation: is the observed drawdown due to the order of the trades? No-edge null: how many systems with no edge at all, but the same sizes of gains and losses, would do as well?

Three simulations, three questions: the bootstrap measures how unlucky the same edge can get; the permutation tests whether performance owes to trade order; the null counts edge-less systems that would do as well.

The “no-edge” null keeps the empirical distributions of gains and losses and only neutralises their frequency (p₀ = 0.3832). It answers: “is the observed hit rate explainable by luck, given this gain/loss profile?” — it is not an efficient-market null.

Scope and limits

How to read this sectionWhat the audit could not see, and what it does not claim to do. These limits are part of the result: a report that did not state them would be misleading.
  • Only closed trades are analysed: floating P&L and open positions are unknown.
  • No price series: stop sensitivity, look-ahead and execution realism untested.
  • The walk-forward relies on results supplied by the client, never replayed.
  • No prop-firm ruleset: no replay.
  • The “no-edge” null keeps the empirical distributions: it is not an efficient-market null.
  • The auditor observes; it recommends no parameter setting.
  • The variance of Sharpe ratios across variants is unknown: Lo correction, conservative.
